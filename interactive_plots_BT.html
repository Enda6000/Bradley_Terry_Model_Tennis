Averaged standard error of player estimates

This is essentially says how much does a players’ performance vary, how consistent of a performer are they? This interpretation is sensible for players with more than 5 games played and tells you who the steady performers are, we can see a strong correlation between the number of games played and the standard error of the a players’ ability/strength score.We can see the top player have the most games played and the lowest standard errors amongst those who have played more than 5 games.

However, for players with stronger shrinkage there is a caveat. Their ability/strength scores will vary less because of how the model is designed with a harsher shrinkage parameter. As a result their standard error is less indicative of the consistency of their performance and more of a consequence of the model.

Network/Comparison Graph as motivation for Maximum A Posteriori Estimation

Why is this network graph of interest? The comparison graph with players as nodes and a directed edge from each match winner to the loser helps determine whether maximum likelihood estimation is even well-defined. If the comparison graph is not strongly connected, meaning every player is linked to every other by a directed path of wins, we risk our estimates diverging when performing MLE. Our graph does not satisfy this condition, so some ability estimates would diverge under pure MLE. This motivated our use of Maximum A Posteriori estimation, whose prior acts as a regulariser and guarantees finite, unique estimates regardless of the graph’s connectivity.

Log loss values as a function of hyperparameters

The plane defined by the x-axis and y-axis is precisely our 2d grid of \(\lambda\) pairs. The z-axis is the corresponding log loss value for a given pair. So this graph is the resulting log loss values as a function of our hyperparameters. Since we want a log loss which is as small as possible this surface is very telling for what values we should choose for our hyperparameters.

The graph here shows that the surface is flat around the minimum which means if we change our shift the value of our stronger shrinkage hyperparameter the log loss we are trying to minimise chanegs very little. The reason for not choosing the absolute minimum but taking a point nearby is explained in more depth in section 3.3 of the full report.
